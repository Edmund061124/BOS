Understanding this custom work

What is the biggest difference between a custom website or platform and a template site?

A template fixes structure and features first, and the business adapts. Custom work starts from your requirements — pages, flows and connections are defined around your business, not the other way around.

What kind of real problem is a custom website mainly meant to solve?

Often the public site does not match how work actually runs — it can display information, but enquiries, records, status or admin work still happen elsewhere by hand. Custom work builds the online parts you specify so they can run as you need.

When does a business need a custom website instead of staying on a ready-made template?

When a template cannot cover your flows, fields, permissions or integrations — or when forcing the template costs almost as much as rebuilding. If you only need a standard brochure with editable text and images, a template is often enough.

What needs to be clear before a custom website scope can be set?

At least: who it is for, which tasks it must complete, which pages or features are required, whether an admin is needed, whether it must connect to existing tools, and what belongs in phase one. Clearer inputs make scope and timing clearer.

After you decide to customise

How does a custom website usually move from confirmed requirements to delivery?

Typical path: discuss requirements → confirm scope → design and build → you confirm key checkpoints → delivery. Each step follows what you confirmed, not a fixed package.

What mainly affects the custom timeline?

Complexity of features, number of integrations, whether materials are ready, and how quickly confirmations come back. Small, clear scope is faster; large mid-project changes extend the timeline.

What does the client need to provide and confirm during custom work?

Usually: how the work should run, necessary copy or assets, access for integrations if any, and confirmation of scope, key screens and acceptance criteria. Build follows what you confirm.

After delivery, how does further custom work continue for redesigns or new features?

Treat new needs as a new scope: state what to change and how far to go, then re-estimate time and fee before continuing. Delivery is not a hard stop — changes are re-aligned first.
